# Source footage, and what the scan found

Nothing in this film was recorded for it. It is cut from the demo takes the
applications already had — 44 clips, about 140 MB — plus the parity suite's
own comparison reel. The one thing that was made for the film is the score.

Before a clip may appear in `shots.json` it has to appear here, with the
result of `film/tools/scan-identity.sh` against it. That script samples frames
across a clip, OCRs each one with tesseract, and greps the text for anything
that identifies the machine or its owner. **It is necessary and not
sufficient**: three of the five clips rejected below passed the grep and were
caught by looking at the frames, which is why every clip used here was also
reviewed as a contact sheet by eye.

## Rendered, not recorded

Chapter four's two frames are not footage. `film/tools/rtlcheck` draws them
through palmy's own text path at 2560x1440 and `film/build.sh` re-renders them
on every build. There is no desktop, no filesystem and no home directory
anywhere near them, and no committed PNG that could drift from what the
toolkit actually does today.

## Camera and microphone

**No frame of this film is camera footage and no audio in it was recorded by a
microphone.** Two independent reasons:

- None of the 44 source clips has an audio stream at all. `ffprobe` reports
  one video stream and nothing else for every one of them, so there is no
  path by which microphone audio could reach the mix. The only audio in the
  film is `film/score/`, synthesised by fluidsynth from MIDI written by
  `film/score/compose.py`.
- The clips that could have carried camera video — `call`, `captions`,
  `video`, `videoedit` — were recorded against synthetic sources, and the
  `call` take says so on screen: "Synthetic pattern (source)", over SMPTE
  colour bars. None of them is used here in any case.

## The scan of the finished film

The gate that actually matters is the output, not the inputs. 350 frames were
extracted from the graded cut at 3 fps, OCRed, and grepped against the same
pattern set: **zero hits**. Every distinct word the OCR found across those
frames was checked by hand and belongs to the project.
